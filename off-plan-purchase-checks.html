BEFORE EACH PAYMENT

Know what you are relying on before you pay.

When much of the price is paid before title is transferred, the contract and the evidence behind each payment matter. We help you gather that evidence and prepare the right questions for our lawyer, who advises on the contract. We cannot guarantee that a developer will perform or repay.

Questions to settle before each payment

  1. 01

    What objective evidence will confirm each construction or approval milestone before an instalment falls due?

  2. 02

    Whether any escrow, bank guarantee or other security protects payments made before transfer.

  3. 03

    Which approvals are already in place and which are still outstanding.

  4. 04

    What happens to your payments if approvals, completion or foreign-ownership registration cannot be achieved.

  5. 05

    How the completion date can be extended, and for what reasons.

How we help

  1. 01

    Obtain the documents and milestone evidence the developer agrees to provide, and record what has and has not been supplied.

  2. 02

    Where agreed, our technical specialists confirm construction progress against the payment schedule.

  3. 03

    Relay the amendment requests you approve and track the developer’s replies, within an agreed mandate.

  4. 04

    Return the revised contract to our lawyer to check that the agreed protections were included before you sign.

  5. 05

    Keep your payment instructions and transfer records organised.

Our lawyer advises on the contract wording, your remedies and which amendments are worth requesting. A refund right is only as useful as the seller’s ability to meet it, so we also help you gather the available information about the project, the seller and its approvals.

VERIFYING PROGRESS

Match what you pay with what is built.

A payment schedule tied to construction stages is only as reliable as the evidence behind each stage. Where you agree it, our technical specialists record the visible progress, so you can compare it with the developer’s payment request before you approve a payment.

Milestone verification at each stage

  1. 01

    Agree the milestones: we list each construction or approval milestone in your payment schedule and the evidence the developer has agreed to provide.

  2. 02

    Visit and record: where agreed, our technical specialist visits the site and records visible progress against that milestone with dated photographs.

  3. 03

    Compare with the payment request: we set the dated record beside the developer’s request, so you can see what is complete before you decide.

  4. 04

    Keep a dated file: photographs, reports and developer replies are kept in one dated file you can share with our lawyer if a question arises.

A site visit records visible progress. It is not an engineering certificate or a legal opinion, and it cannot guarantee that a developer will complete the project or repay. Our lawyer advises on your contract rights.

WARNING SIGNS

Signs worth raising early.

Projects rarely fail without some warning. These are the signals we help you record, so that you can decide what to do next with advice from our lawyer.

What to watch for

  1. 01

    Repeated postponement of the construction start or completion date.

  2. 02

    Payment requests that are not linked to a milestone you can see.

  3. 03

    Approvals or permits that are promised but not shown.

  4. 04

    Site activity that has slowed or stopped for several weeks.

  5. 05

    Slow or unclear answers about the land, approvals or financing of the project.

If you notice these signs, we record the current status and the developer’s explanation and share them with our lawyer. We cannot make a developer perform or refund. Where a dispute needs legal action, our lawyer advises under a separate legal engagement.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE

A dated file you can act on.

You receive the documents the developer provided, a list of what is still outstanding and, where included, dated site photographs and our technical specialist’s findings for each milestone.

We share the file with our lawyer, so the contract advice rests on evidence rather than assumption.

WHAT TO KNOW

Clear about what we can and cannot do.

We gather evidence, record progress and follow up. Our lawyer advises on the contract, your remedies and which amendments are worth requesting, under a separate legal engagement. Our technical specialists provide the agreed inspections.

Share what you already have.

  • Your reservation or sale and purchase agreement, or the relevant clauses
  • The payment schedule and which instalments are already paid
  • Any construction updates, photographs or notices from the developer

A short description is enough to begin. We confirm the documents needed and how to share them after discussing your scope.

SCOPE AND COSTS

Agree the work before it begins.

Your total quote includes Baanward’s fee and all legal and technical specialist fees for the agreed work. We confirm access, visits, specialist availability, reports, timing and any travel costs. Extra work or spending outside that scope requires your approval.

When should I contact you?+

Before you sign a reservation or booking agreement or pay a non-refundable deposit, and in any case before the next instalment falls due. Earlier contact leaves time to review the documents.

Is a title check worth it if the contract already promises clear title?+

A check shows the position on the day it is made and cannot stop a later change by itself. It still tells you what you are relying on today and gives our lawyer a dated starting point for comparing the position before your final payment. Our lawyer advises whether a further check is worthwhile before you pay the last instalment.

What evidence can I ask for before an instalment?+

Typically the developer’s written confirmation of the milestone, the relevant approval documents and, where agreed, a dated site visit by our technical specialist. Our lawyer advises what your contract entitles you to request.

Can you tell me whether my payments are protected?+

We ask the developer whether any escrow, bank guarantee or other security applies and record the answer. Our lawyer advises what it means under your contract.

What should I do if my project is delayed again?+

Ask us to record the current status and the developer’s written explanation, and we share them with our lawyer. The contract decides whether the delay can be extended and which remedies you have.

Can someone visit the site if I am abroad?+

Yes, where agreed and where the developer allows access. We arrange the visit, our technical specialist records visible progress and you receive dated photographs and notes.
