COSTS OF BUYING

Costs of Buying Property in Thailand
and who usually pays.

Beyond the price, a purchase in Thailand has fees and taxes charged at transfer. This page explains the main ones, who usually pays and how the figures are worked out, with an illustrative example. Rates and temporary reductions change, so our lawyer confirms the figures for your transfer.

WHERE BUYERS ARE SURPRISED

Four things to ask before you agree.

  1. 01

    Who pays is negotiable

    The contract decides the split. Read it before you agree the price, not at the Land Office.

  2. 02

    Off-plan prices

    Developers often quote a net price that includes some of these costs and passes others on to you. Ask for the full list in writing.

  3. 03

    Value versus price

    The fee is based on the appraised value and the taxes on the higher of price and value, so a low stated price does not remove them.

  4. 04

    Condo extras

    Ask about the sinking fund, advance common fees and any special assessment due at transfer.

QUESTIONS

Costs of buying, in plain terms.

Who pays the transfer fee in Thailand?+

It is usually shared between buyer and seller, often equally, but the contract decides. Developers sometimes cover it in the price. Ask for the split in writing before you reserve.

Do foreign buyers get a reduced transfer fee?+

Temporary reductions have been announced from time to time, with conditions on the buyer and the price that may exclude foreign buyers. Ask our lawyer which rate applies at your transfer.

How much cash do I need on transfer day?+

The agreed share of the transfer fee, any mortgage registration, prepaid common fees and the sinking fund, plus the balance of the price in the form the contract requires. We help you list these in advance.

Can a lower registered price reduce the cost?+

No. The fee and the taxes are based on the appraised value as well as the price, and stating a lower price than the real one is a legal and contractual risk. Ask our lawyer.

Does Baanward calculate these costs?+

We help you list the expected costs inside your agreed scope, and our lawyer confirms the figures. They are estimates until the Land Office confirms them at transfer.
